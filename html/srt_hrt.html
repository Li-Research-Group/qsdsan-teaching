SRT & HRT: how does solids retention time affect effluent quality?

Interactive explorer for an activated sludge process, built on QSDsan's steady-state ActivatedSludgeProcess model (Rittmann & McCarty kinetics). Solids retention time (SRT) is the average time the treatment biomass spends in the system. Too short an SRT and biomass is washed out faster than it can grow — there's a theoretical minimum SRT (SRT_min) below which treatment always collapses. Drag the SRT slider down until it happens, then back up to watch effluent COD fall and flatten out.

*BOD5 permit limit shown against modeled COD for scale only — not an exact compliance check.

Try it yourself
  • At what SRT does the system wash out? Does that match the vertical dashed SRT_min line?
  • Hold SRT fixed and move only S0. Effluent COD barely moves compared to dragging SRT — SRT dominates. At steady state, effluent substrate concentration in a CSTR-with-recycle is set mainly by SRT and kinetics (your chosen SRT pins the biomass growth rate via μ = 1/SRT + b). What changes instead as S0 rises? Watch HRT and WAS production.
  • Real plants typically operate at 5–20× (or more) above SRT_min, even though effluent COD looks "good enough" much closer to the minimum. This module only models BOD/COD removal — what other goals might require a longer SRT?
Every number here comes from a real QSDsan ActivatedSludgeProcess simulation, precomputed offline across a grid (not reimplemented in JavaScript) — see scripts/precompute_asp_grid.py in the qsdsan-teaching repo. SRT is swept in whole days only because the underlying model rounds SRT internally; S0 is linearly interpolated between precomputed grid points; WAS production is scaled exactly by Q (it scales linearly with flow at fixed SRT/S0, confirmed by the repo's test suite) rather than gridded.
